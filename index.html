THE CORE IDEA

Keep the algorithm deterministic along each sample path.
Let stochasticity do the global exploration.

The method replaces periodic extremum-seeking dithers by McShane-type smooth approximations of Wiener paths. Each realization remains an ordinary differential equation on the manifold, but its small-\(\varepsilon\) behavior approaches a Riemannian Langevin diffusion whose Gibbs law concentrates near global minimizers.

MAIN ALGORITHM · EQ. (7)

Once the driving path \(W\) is fixed, the dynamics are deterministic. Randomness enters through the smooth Brownian approximation, while the objective appears only through measurements of \(\phi(x)\).

WONG–ZAKAI LIMIT

The random ODE behaves like Riemannian Langevin dynamics.

With the compensation drift chosen in Theorem 2, the limiting Stratonovich SDE has the same infinitesimal generator as a Riemannian Langevin diffusion.

\[L\psi=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\Delta\psi-\langle\mathrm{grad}\,\phi,\mathrm{grad}\,\psi\rangle_g\]
Gradient descent term\(-\mathrm{grad}\,\phi\) drives the process downhill.
Brownian exploration\(\kappa^{-1/2}dB^g\) keeps the process moving across the manifold.
Exact Gibbs law\(\mu_\kappa=c_\kappa^{-1}e^{-2\kappa\phi}\,\mathrm{vol}\).
ENERGY LANDSCAPE → GIBBS LAW

Increase \(\kappa\), and mass migrates toward the global minimizers.

For every closed set separated from \(S^\star\), the Gibbs probability decays exponentially in \(\kappa\). The tradeoff is familiar from annealing: larger \(\kappa\) gives sharper concentration, but mixing can become slower.

THEOREM 3

The algorithm itself inherits the Gibbs behavior over long horizons.

Sampling the random ODE at \(t=k\varepsilon\) produces a time-homogeneous Markov chain. For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\), its transition laws converge uniformly toward the Gibbs measure in bounded-Lipschitz distance after a sufficiently long time.

Infinite-horizon statement
\[\sup_{x\in\mathcal M}d_{BL}\!\left(P_\varepsilon^k(x,\cdot),\mu_\kappa\right)\le \delta\]

In one sentence

A payoff-only random ODE on a compact manifold is designed so that, in the small-interpolation limit, it behaves like Riemannian Langevin dynamics and therefore acquires global probabilistic optimization guarantees through a Gibbs invariant measure.
